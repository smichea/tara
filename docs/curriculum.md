# Curriculum: guided practice from first words to nuanced expression

Tara contains **124 Filipino lessons**, **576 key expressions**, **112 readings**,
**224 comprehension questions** and **112 open expression tasks**. There are 52
general language lessons (16 A1, 8 A2, 8 B1, 8 B2, 8 C1, 4 C2), plus 72 school
pathway lessons: Foundations, every Grade 2–7, and Grade 8. School lessons also
carry editorial language-practice targets; those are not grade equivalences.
The first 12 lessons and their entry IDs are preserved for existing progress.

## Reference and limits

The Council of Europe's [CEFR level descriptions](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/level-descriptions)
provide language-independent reference goals. Its [descriptor framework](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors)
and [mediation guidance](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/mediation)
inform the move from isolated exchanges to connected discourse, interpretation,
synthesis and explaining ideas for another reader.

This is an **editorial adaptation**, not a validated CEFR alignment or an
examination. A practice label is a target, not the level a learner has achieved.
These guided lessons do not constitute all the sustained input, interaction,
feedback and assessment required to demonstrate advanced proficiency. The C2
section introduces demanding guided tasks; completion does not certify C2.

Except for two attributed public-domain Balagtas stanzas, passages, examples,
questions and model responses were authored for Tara.
They are drafts awaiting fluent-speaker and teacher review. They do not reproduce
a proprietary textbook. The vocabulary and grammatical structures have not been
independently audited against a Filipino reference-level syllabus.

## Sequence

| Target | Focus of guided practice | Expression and review |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Introductions, everyday needs, time, quantities, descriptions and classroom instructions | Brief messages and ordered instructions |
| A2 | Habits, completed actions, plans, reasons, requests and practical messages | Connected short descriptions and useful replies |
| B1 | Narrative sequence, causes, sentence roles, main ideas and source attribution | Summaries, supported views and coherent paragraphs |
| B2 | Argument, counterargument, media purpose, conditions, register and literary evidence | Reasoned proposals and interpretations with limitations |
| C1 | Implication, qualification, synthesis, irony, narrative perspective, mediation and revision | Thesis-led analysis and audience-sensitive explanations |
| C2 | Ambiguity, competing explanations, delicate mediation and a critical portfolio | Weighing interpretations, preserving uncertainty and reflecting on revisions |

The verb-aspect explanation was cross-checked against the author-maintained
[Learning Tagalog aspect overview](https://learningtagalog.com/grammar/verbs/aspects/overview.html).
Examples in Tara are original; no exercise set or commercial lesson is copied.

## Learning and assessment behavior

- Key expressions are taught before recall practice. Filipino politeness additions
  and declared answer variants remain supported.
- Readings provide optional English support. Browser TTS is used only for English
  in generated reverse courses; stored
  audio requires named pronunciation review. See [audio workflow](audio.md).
- Reading questions test specific details, interpretation or argument structure,
  with explanations. They do not become vocabulary flashcards.
- Written tasks accept the learner's own wording. The model is one possible
  response, not a required answer. Self-check criteria guide revision.
- Written responses are stored in localStorage and included in backups. Teachers
  or fluent readers can give feedback by looking at the learner's work; no automatic
  language-quality or school-grade scoring is claimed.
- Existing streaks, XP and completion records survive the course expansion.
  Completion is practice activity, not a measured proficiency credential.

## Further review priorities

Prioritize natural Filipino usage, alternative phrasings, consistent politeness,
readability, progression between adjacent lessons, ambiguity in answer options,
and whether productive tasks are supported by enough examples. The destination
is Grade 8 DepEd/MATATAG; the [school mapping](school-alignment.md) includes all
earlier stages while identifying where teacher assessment and fuller assigned
reading are still needed.
